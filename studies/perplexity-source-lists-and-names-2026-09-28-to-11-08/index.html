How often did Perplexity name a business when its website appeared in the source list?

Perplexity was the sole API engine with readable source lists in this record. All 2,014 readable local-market answers had 15 source entries from 2026-09-28 to 2026-11-08. Across 8,766 OK Claude, Gemini and OpenAI API rows from 2026-09-28 to 2026-11-23, none had a nonempty source list.

We paired eligible domains seen in Google's organic results on 5 or more days with readable Perplexity answers for the same market: 7,812 business-days, 35 markets, 2026-09-28 to 2026-11-08.

  • A URL on the business's domain was listed: 1,300 business-days. Named on 371 (28.5%), 2026-09-28 to 2026-11-08.
  • No URL on its domain was listed: 6,512 business-days. Named on 138 (2.1%), same window.
  • Of the 509 named business-days, 371 (72.9%) were listed, same window.

A listed page goes with a name far more often, not every time. On 929 of 1,300 listed business-days (71.5%), the recorded names did not match the business under the strict rule. Across the 7 trades with 100 or more listed business-days the strict naming rate runs from 9.6% to 50.0% (2026-09-28 to 2026-11-08). This is an association in our rows, not a test of cause.

In API rows with no recorded source list, the strict rule matched eligible businesses: Gemini 237 of 4,126 business-days (5.7%), OpenAI 88 of 7,082 (1.2%), Claude 3 of 7,971 (0.0%), 2026-09-28 to 2026-11-09. On 2026-11-10 we changed which model we asked in our OpenAI collection. From 2026-11-10 to 2026-11-23, the strict rule matched 19 of 364 business-days (5.2%).

What the mentions-versus-citations counts do not show

Window of every figure below, unless a line gives its own: 2026-09-28 to 2026-11-08 (Perplexity source lists readable).

Cause

The counts show that a business was named on 371 of 1,300 business-days (28.5%) when a URL on its domain appeared in the source list and on 138 of 6,512 (2.1%) when no URL on its domain appeared, 2026-09-28 to 2026-11-08. They do not show that listing a page causes a name. The returned list and the extracted names can both reflect how closely a business matches the question. The record does not show which listed pages were used to generate the answer. This comparison uses archived observations. It cannot estimate the effect of adding or changing a page on whether a business is named.

What "listed as a source" means

Every readable Perplexity row lists exactly 15 sources (2,014 of 2,014 rows, 2026-09-28 to 2026-11-08). That is the set of pages returned with the answer. We did not test which of them the answer text links or quotes, so "cited" here means listed, not linked inside the answer. A business-day counts as listed when a URL on the business's domain is in that set. Ownership of the domain is not verified.

Which engines

The Perplexity API series supplies the readable source lists for this comparison. Across 8,766 OK Claude, Gemini and OpenAI API rows from 2026-09-28 to 2026-11-23, none had a nonempty source list. These counts show that extracted names can match eligible businesses on API rows that have no recorded source list. The denominators use each engine's available answers. From 2026-09-28 to 2026-11-09, the strict counts were Claude 3 of 7,971 business-days, Gemini 237 of 4,126 and OpenAI 88 of 7,082. On 2026-11-10 we changed which model we asked in our OpenAI collection. From 2026-11-10 to 2026-11-23, the strict rule matched 19 of 364 business-days in that series. The two OpenAI windows are reported separately.

Google AI Overview

We report capture and reference counts only. The archive held readable text for 284 of the 1,246 captured overviews from 2026-09-28 to 2026-11-23. Of the 1,710 Google result rows, 1,246 carried a captured overview and 1,081 of those listed at least one reference (2026-09-28 to 2026-11-23). Naming is not measured because the text is withheld for 962 of the 1,246 overview rows. The capture is a mechanical parse of a results page that can miss an overview; the counts are not how often Google shows an overview.

Rows with no readable sources

Perplexity source lists are withheld from 2026-11-09 under the 2026-11-08 policy: 1,050 of 3,064 local-market answer rows (34.3%), 2026-09-28 to 2026-11-23. Those rows are in the answer count and excluded from every cross-tab. There is no data for 2026-10-05.

Naming

A business counts as named when its normalized domain label matches an extracted name, including matches after generic and market words are removed (strict), or matches by prefix (loose: 42.7% against 3.5%, 2026-09-28 to 2026-11-08). The extracted list is machine-made from answer text, includes headings and place names, and misses names that differ from the web address, such as a person's name. Both rules can miss names and can match strings that are not business names. They use the same procedure on listed and unlisted days, and the error rates may differ. In the business-day comparison, listed URLs assign the exposure group. The name match uses the eligible domain label and the recorded names.

Who is in the pairs

Only businesses that sat in Google's organic results for the same question on 5 or more days in the window (2026-09-28 to 2026-11-08): 205 eligible market-domain combinations across 35 trade-and-city questions, producing 7,812 business-days. Of 2,014 readable answer rows, 1,224 had an eligible market set and entered the comparison. A business Google does not show is not in the pairs. The 35 markets are trade-and-city questions, one fixed wording each, asked through the API; consumer apps can differ. The trade mix is uneven and the rate by trade runs from 9.6% to 50.0% across the 7 trades with 100 or more listed business-days.

Repeats

Business-days of one business are not independent. The CSV's 95% intervals assume independent observations. Repeated businesses, markets and answers can make those intervals too narrow. The intervals do not account for that dependence. Of 115 market-domain pairs with both listed and unlisted days, 33 were named more often on listed days, 7 less often and 75 equally often (2026-09-28 to 2026-11-08).

The 2026-11-03 figures

The email figures (location page cited, named on 106 of 239 days, 44.4%; homepage cited, 130 of 337, 38.6%; 2026-10-02 to 2026-10-24) are not reproduced exactly. The recount for 2026-10-02 to 2026-10-24 finds 324 homepage business-days against 337, with 40.4% named under the loose rule (27.2% under the strict rule). The recount assigns a business-day to the homepage group if any URL on its domain is a homepage. The remaining 421 listed business-days had inner pages only. Of those, 42.5% matched under the loose rule and 23.5% under the strict rule. The recount did not isolate location pages or establish why the earlier totals differ. Do not reuse the 2026-11-03 figures; use these.

Reproducing the counts

The script requires the local domain-classification file used for this count. The input defines excluded directory, marketplace, national-brand and other domains, along with private normalization terms. The public script contains no private classification entries. Reproducing the counts requires this private file and eligible archive inputs.
