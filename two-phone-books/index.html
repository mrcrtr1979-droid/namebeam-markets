There are two phone books now.

AI assistants and Google name almost entirely different local businesses. We counted for 28 days across 22 US markets. 90% of named businesses appear in only one universe.

AS ABOVE, SO BELOW. 3 6 9.
90%

live in a single universe

Of 248 named businesses in the 22 local markets (Perplexity's per-market top-10 and Google's per-market top-10, non-business phrases removed), 223 (89.9%) appear in only one of the two lists and 25 appear in both. Some of the most AI-dominant businesses in the corpus sit outside Google's top-10 entirely: Arrington Roofing in Dallas, named by Perplexity on 24 days, never appears in Google's most-cited list for Dallas. Others live in both books: Google also cited the sites of All Star Movers (Pleasanton, 10 days), Reliable Heating & Air (Atlanta, 10 days) and Gates Enterprises (Denver, its site gatesroof.com, 6 days).

So what: every SEO agency on earth is still optimizing for one phone book. The second phone book has different winners, and nobody is reading it. AI answers are 3 to 5 names, not 10 blue links, second place in AI is tenth place in Google.

Method note: the comparison uses published top-10 lists per market with case-insensitive name normalization, and even that understates the mess. Perplexity's own Denver top-10 lists both "Cenco Roofing" and "CENCO Roofing" (same business, counted twice), while Google sometimes cites domains (e.g. allstarmoverscal.com) rather than business names. Normalization debt lives inside our own lists too. The directional finding, two largely disjoint winner sets, is robust to that. Everything here is computed from the September corpus; the 24 per-market reports are public and every figure above can be checked against them.
