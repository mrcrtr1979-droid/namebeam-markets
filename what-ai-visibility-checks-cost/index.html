What AI visibility checks cost and what each one records

Last updated 2026-11-12. Every price on this page was read from the vendor's own public page on 2026-11-12 between 02:30AM and 02:33AM CT by a plain page fetch with no account, and is printed here as that page printed it. Prices change; the source link on each row is the page to confirm.

An AI visibility check answers one question: when a buyer asks an AI engine for a business, a product or a brand, is yours named? The tools below sell that check in different units: tracked prompts, credits, engines, projects. This page lists what each one prints as its price and what it says it tracks, then describes what the Namebeam record keeps, because the two kinds of product are not the same thing.

What the Namebeam record keeps

  • Per city and trade. Each market is one US city and one trade, with one fixed buyer question written in plain words (for example a Kansas City health insurance market asks who can help pick a plan). The question is printed verbatim on each record page.
  • Which engine named which business, dated. On each run day the same question goes to OpenAI, Anthropic, Perplexity and Gemini by API and to Google search. The record keeps, per source and per day, the business names found in the answer, the status of the call (OK, QUOTA_BLOCKED, FAILED, EXTRACTION_FAILED) and the SHA-256 of the raw answer file. Refusals are logged, not dropped.
  • The prompts used. The user prompt is the market's question, word for word; there is no system prompt. Model ids, modes (web search on or off) and run times are stated on the pages that report them.
  • Window and size. The public record covers 33 markets from 2026-08-05 to 2026-11-12 (data file generated 2026-11-13): 13,252 rows, 10,742 of them with an answer logged. Each record page carries a free CSV and links to the raw files in the public repository.
  • A sample, not a verdict. One answer on one day is a sample. The record pages compare each run day with the previous run day on the engines that answered both days, so a name that appears or drops is shown with its dates. Nothing on these pages ranks a business or explains why an engine answered as it did.

Two dated readings from the record, with their denominators. Across 55 local buyer questions and 17 run days from 2026-10-24 to 2026-11-09, any two of three engines (ChatGPT gpt-5-mini, Claude haiku 4.5 on Bedrock, Gemini flash lite) shared at least one business name in 1,070 of 1,916 engine pairs, 55.8 percent (the study). On 2026-11-11 (UTC), five runs per market with web search off across 28 markets and 12 trades, gemini-3.5-flash-lite named at least one business in 134 of 140 runs; claude-haiku-4-5 named one in 8 of 140, and those 8 were national insurance carriers in one insurance market and three runs in one towing market (trade pages). API answers; consumer apps can differ.

How to read the table

  • Units differ. A prompt tracked daily on one engine is one unit at some tools and 31 credits a month at others (Otterly's own FAQ says so). Compare the unit before the price.
  • Engines differ. Each page names its own engine list and which plans include which engines. A check that leaves out the engine your buyers use is a check of something else.
  • A record is not a dashboard. The tools above show a dashboard of tracked prompts for your own brand. The Namebeam record is a public, dated file of what each engine answered for one buyer question per market, kept whether or not anyone is paying for that market, with every refusal logged. The free check reads that record for your city and trade and promises no result.
  • Read more than one day. Whichever tool you use, one answer on one day is a sample. The record pages show the day-to-day changes with dates so that a month can be compared, not a day.
